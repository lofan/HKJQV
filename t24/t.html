Foundational Pillars

Zhong Ding (Central Equilibrium)

Central equilibrium forms the stability axis of the 24-form, referring to a centered, vertically aligned posture that keeps the body's axis perfectly stable during weight shifts and turns. This ensures balance without rigidity, allowing you to root down securely into the ground while remaining highly dynamic and responsive.

  • Keep the spine elongated, pulling the crown of your head up gently (Suspended Crown).
  • Tuck the tailbone slightly (drop the sacrum) to relieve lower back curvature.
  • Coordinate step movements such that foot transitions occur along clear, stable tracks.
Energetics & Release

Song (Active Relaxation)

Active relaxation (Song) is distinct from limpness. It involves consciously releasing unnecessary tension in muscles, joints, and tendons, enabling standard structural pathways to support weight. Relaxing open the joints allows internal energy (Qi) to flow and sink to the Dantien.

  • Let the shoulders sink naturally; do not shrug.
  • Keep elbows heavy and pointing downward to avoid arm tension.
  • Breathe from the diaphragm, letting your lower abdomen rise and fall.
Dual Nature

Yin-Yang Dynamics

Tai Chi is the external manifestation of internal duality. Every movement contains weight-bearing (Yang) and empty (Yin) sides, forward extension (Yang) and withdrawal/holding (Yin). Learning to separate the substantial and insubstantial weight distribution prevents sluggish knee joints.

  • Ensure one leg is fully weighted (Yang) before taking a step with the unweighted leg (Yin).
  • Keep the mind calm (Yin) even while directing physically active force (Yang).
Breath Harmony

Breathing Guidelines

Coordinating breathing with postures deepens practice and activates the parasympathetic system. The standard rules are simple but take careful application:

  • Inhale: When pulling arms in, lifting upward, or closing/storing force.
  • Exhale: When pushing, pressing forward, kicking, or driving force outward.
  • Keep the breath continuous, slow, and soft, matching the tempo of the limbs.
